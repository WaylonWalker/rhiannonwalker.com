## Emotional Rollercoaster 

Just when I feel I have my feet under me there's always something.  As a mother I'd love to put blinders on and believe both kids are the same as everyone else, but I'd be the one hurting.  All it takes is one comment or action to be reminded raising these kids will never be the typical road.  It always seems to hit hard when you finally feel stable.  Today it was a comment from Wyatt referencing timeframe his eyes have been bothering him.  From the view point of the nurse here sits a teen old enough to fully answer his own questions.  He has a mustache for fuck sake.   As soon as he answered "years" the punch was thrown.   I jumped on and answered in the appropriate time, 3 weeks and it's progressive getting worse.   Thoughts in my mind are running their own conversation. "Do they think I'm making this up?  Am I overreacting?    Maybe he's right.  No, I know it's only been a few weeks.  Well, that's a sign right there that he's developing slower.". All playing at hyper speed and bringing on lots of feelings.  

Nothing makes coping with the knowledge of having a child that most likely won't leave your house at 18 easy.  Of course we want to give him the life he needs.  No, I won't put him in a facility.  I do believe he will be able to hold down a job as an adult.  It's the emotional and hygiene stuff I'm worried about. I'm terrified someone will take advantage of his empathy and use him.  I'm terrified that he will struggle with the line between a relationship and being used.  

I don't know why this is all hitting, but it's really messing with my mind.  The reminders are there daily.  How many 13 year olds do you hear call their Mom's "Mama"  still? Don't get me wrong , I'd be sad to not ever hear it again, but the facts are just that.  

When he came home today and screamed at everyone else. He came in our room and used me as a way to regulate. He was completely different person walking out. I on the other hand felt drained. I'll always and have always been his safe space. 

A safe space for the rest of my life.  One that will always be in close proximity and not have the years post kids. That will be a phase of life we will never know.  I hear the adjustment phase to that is hard, but then you feel your relationship with your spouse reunite.  

I guess Waylon and I will need to figure out our own special way to grow when he becomes an age where he's essentially living out of our basement as a rented apartment.  Maybe that's the answer.  He Grandma Sally's it.
